Insulation That Keeps Your Home Comfortable Year-Round.

Proper insulation is one of the most effective ways to improve your home’s comfort and energy efficiency. In winter, insulation helps keep heated air inside. In summer, it helps slow heat from entering your living space—reducing strain on your heating and cooling systems. Why insulation matters

Insulation helps protect your home from outdoor temperatures.

In winter, heat tries to escape from your home. In summer, outdoor heat tries to enter. Insulation slows this movement and helps indoor temperatures stay more comfortable.

Good insulation can reduce hot and cold rooms, help your heating and cooling system work less, and make the whole home feel more even.

More even comfortRooms stay closer to the temperature you choose.
Less HVAC workYour heating and cooling system may not need to run as often.
“EPA estimates that homeowners can save an average of 15% on heating and cooling costs (or an average of 11% on total energy costs) by air sealing their homes and adding insulation in attics, floors over crawl spaces and basements.”
— ENERGY STAR, “Why Seal and Insulate?”

These are modeled national averages. Savings for an individual home will vary based on its condition, climate, energy use, and completed improvements.

Common insulation materials

Common types of home insulation.

The right type depends on where it will be installed, how easy the area is to reach, and the condition of your home.

There is no single best insulation for every home.

Good results depend on choosing the right material, installing it correctly, sealing air leaks, and reaching the needed R-value.

Compare materials →

Blown-In Fiberglass

Loose glass fibers spread evenly across an area. This form of fiberglass is often used to add insulation to attic floors.

Blown-In Cellulose

Loose insulation made mostly from recycled paper fiber and treated for fire resistance. It fills around framing and works well in many attics and walls.

Fiberglass Batts

Pre-cut or rolled glass-fiber insulation placed between wall, floor, or ceiling framing. It must fit correctly without gaps.

Dense-Pack

Dense-pack insulation is blown under pressure into enclosed wall, floor, and ceiling cavities to fill gaps and improve thermal performance. It’s especially useful in older Maryland homes with little or no insulation because it can often be installed through small access holes without removing large areas of drywall or plaster.

Spray Foam

Foam mixed and sprayed at the home. Open-cell and closed-cell foam have different uses, and careful installation is important.

Rigid Foam Board

Firm insulation panels used in some walls, foundations, basements, and rim joists.

Warning signs

Signs your home may need more insulation.

Hot or cold rooms

Upstairs rooms, additions, or rooms above garages may feel different from the rest of the home.

Cold walls or floors

Cold surfaces and large temperature changes between floors can be signs of missing insulation.

Heating or cooling runs often

Your system may be working harder because heat is entering or leaving too quickly.

Insulation looks thin or damaged

Gaps, flattened material, water stains, or pest damage can make insulation less effective.

Risks of poor insulation

Poor insulation can lead to bigger home problems.

Missing or damaged insulation can affect comfort, energy bills, moisture, and the life of your heating and cooling system.

Problems to take seriously

  • Rooms that stay too hot or too cold.
  • Higher heating and cooling costs.
  • Extra wear on your HVAC system.
  • Condensation when warm, damp air touches a cold surface.
  • Roof ice dams caused partly by heat escaping into the attic.
  • Wet insulation, odors, and damage to nearby materials.

Where insulation belongs

Insulation should surround the living areas of your home.

It works best when there are no missing sections or weak spots.

Attics and rooflines

Heat can move quickly through the top of a home when insulation is missing or uneven.

Exterior walls

Empty or poorly insulated walls can cause cold surfaces and uncomfortable rooms.

Floors and foundations

Basements, crawlspaces, rim joists, and garages can make the floors above them uncomfortable.

Knee walls

Short walls beside finished attic rooms often have gaps or missing insulation.

Insulation basics

Common homeowner questions.

What is R-value?

R-value tells you how well insulation slows heat. A higher number means more insulating power. Good results also depend on correct installation, full coverage, and keeping the insulation dry. The ENERGY STAR chart below shows general recommendations by climate area.

Why should air leaks be sealed first?

Insulation slows heat, but air sealing stops drafts through cracks and holes. Sealing those leaks first helps the insulation work better.

Can new insulation go over old insulation?

Often, yes. The old insulation should be checked first for water, pests, dirt, damage, and poor installation.

Does insulation stop drafts?

Not always. Air sealing is usually needed to close the cracks and holes that cause drafts.

Can insulation get damaged?

Yes. Water, pests, storage, foot traffic, and repair work can move or damage insulation and make it work less effectively.
